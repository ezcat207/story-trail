# The Ending: A Chest with Two Locks
*4 min read*

**The picture:** A treasure chest with two locks needs two keys. A good ending needs two things to click.

**Lock 1: the action ending.** The outside problem is solved or settled. The war is stopped, the race is won, the secret is out.

**Lock 2: the meaning ending.** The reader sees how the hero changed, or what the story was really about. A look, a quiet moment, a small action can say it.

**The rules**
- **Tie the loose threads.** If you introduced something, finish it or deliberately leave it open.
- **Do not explain the moral out loud.** Show the hero doing something differently than they did at the start.
- **Do not drag on.** After the climax, a few paragraphs to a couple of pages. Not ten.
- **Echo the opening.** Return to an image from the beginning, but changed. This gives readers a satisfying circle.
- **Last image matters.** Readers remember the beginning and the end most. Make the last line count.
- **Sequel hooks are okay,** but the story in front of you must finish first.

**Percy Jackson check:** The quest ends with the bolt returned and the war avoided. Then there is a quieter beat about family and where Percy belongs now. The outside problem is solved, the inside one starts to heal, and a hint at a bigger future is left for the next book.

**Try it (20 min):** Reread your first page. Write a last paragraph that echoes it with one thing changed.
