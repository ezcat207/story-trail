# Do You Need an Outline?
*5 min read*

**The picture:** Some people hike with a printed route. Some people follow their nose. Both can get to the top. But the ones with no route should still carry a compass (that is your one-sentence story).

**What an outline actually is:** a list of the big moments, in order, so you can see if the story holds together before you spend 20 hours writing it.

**Pick your style**
- **Plotter:** you outline first. Good if you tend to lose the thread.
- **Pantser:** you write by feel. Good if outlines make you freeze. You must plan to rewrite more later.
- **Hybrid (try this first):** a loose skeleton, with five to ten beats, and room to wander.

**How to build a quick outline**
1. Write the one-sentence story.
2. Write 300 words of summary: beginning, middle, end.
3. Break it into 8 to 15 beats, each one sentence.
4. For each beat, add: *what does the hero want here? what goes wrong?*
5. Mark the biggest turn (the middle) and the big fight (near the end).

If a new idea shows up while writing, ask: does it help the main line? If yes, keep it. If not, put it in an "idea box" for another story.

**Percy Jackson check:** The book's route is easy to map: learn about the gods, get the quest, travel across the country, hit a dark moment, face the villain, return. Not a surprise to the author. The surprises are the monsters, jokes, and twists he put on the road.

**Try it (20 min):** Write your 8-beat outline on index cards. Then shuffle two cards and see if the story gets better or worse.
