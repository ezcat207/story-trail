# Revise and Score: Be Your Own Coach
*6 min read*

**The picture:** An athlete watches game film. Not to feel bad, but to find what to train. Revision is your film session.

**The process**
1. **Finish the draft first.** Do not polish page one forever.
2. **Rest.** A day or two away helps you see it fresh.
3. **Read aloud.** Where did you trip? Get bored? Get confused?
4. **Score it** with the rubric on the Score My Story tab.
5. **Fix the top one to three problems only.** Big things first (structure, stakes, climax), small things last (word choice, commas).
6. **Re-score.** Did the number go up for the right reasons?
7. **Share with a real person** and ask one specific question, like "Where did you get lost?"

**Using AI the right way**
- Paste your story into an AI with the rubric prompt. It will give feedback and a score, and it should not rewrite your story.
- Disagree sometimes. If the feedback is wrong, say why. That is you being the author.
- Tell readers if AI helped you with feedback.

**What scores mean**
- 60–75: a solid first draft.
- 76–85: strong and clear.
- 86+: rare. Be proud.

**Percy Jackson check:** Even a famous book went through drafts. Notice the places in the book that made you laugh, gasp, or care. Those are the parts to study.

**Try it (20 min):** Score your story yourself from 1–5 for each of the eight rubric areas before you ask the AI. Compare. Where do you disagree?
