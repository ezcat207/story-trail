# The Opening: Start Where Things Change
*5 min read*

**The picture:** Walking into a party, you decide in about ten seconds whether to stay. Readers do the same with your first page. Start at the doorway of the loud room, not in the parking lot.

**The rules**
- **Start at the moment normal breaks.** Not at birth, not at "a regular Tuesday," not with the hero waking up and brushing their teeth.
- **Raise a question in the first 200 words or so.** Who is that? Why is she running? What is in the box?
- **Show a hint of normal life through action,** not a lecture. One telling detail beats a paragraph of backstory.
- **Make it clear whose story this is.** Readers need someone to hold onto.
- **Promise something.** The opening tells the reader what kind of ride this is: funny, scary, strange.

**Common ways to wreck an opening**
1. A weather report or long description before anything happens.
2. An info dump about the world, history, or magic rules.
3. Too many names in the first page.
4. Starting too early, with ten pages before the real trouble.
5. A fake-out ("it was all a dream") that cheats the reader.

**Where to cut in:** Write your opening, then ask: could I cut the first page and lose nothing? If yes, cut it.

**Percy Jackson check:** Percy's story begins with a school trip going wrong, and with the narrator warning the reader: if you think you might be a demigod, close this book. It sets voice, humor, and danger in a few lines. Then a teacher turns into a monster. Normal life breaks quickly.

**Try it (20 min):** Take your first paragraph. Move the story's first real problem into the first five sentences. Read it aloud.
