# Collecting Ideas: Fill Your Pantry
*4 min read*

**The picture:** A chef does not invent dinner from nothing at 6 p.m. They have a pantry. Your head needs a pantry too. Ideas come when you have stuff on the shelves.

**The rules**
- **Carry a catcher.** Notes app, voice memos, a pocket notebook. An idea you do not catch is gone in about ninety seconds.
- **Collect three kinds of things:** weird facts ("a town in Norway where it is against the law to die"), real moments (an argument you overheard), and "what if" questions ("what if the school cafeteria menu was a secret code?").
- **Do not judge while collecting.** Judging is a different job for a different day.
- **Mix two unrelated things.** A lighthouse plus a pizza delivery. A ghost plus a group chat. The collision is where stories start.
- **Steal the feeling, not the plot.** If a movie made your stomach drop, ask what made it drop. Use that trick in your own world.

**Percy Jackson check:** The big idea is a mix. Take ancient Greek gods. Drop them in modern New York. Make a kid with ADHD and dyslexia the hero, and explain those as signs of his demigod brain. Old myths plus a today-kid plus a clever twist equals a pantry-ingredient mash-up.

**Try it (20 min):** Write 10 "what if" lines in a row without stopping. Then circle two that could mash together.
