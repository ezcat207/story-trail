# Hooks: Make Them Turn the Page
*5 min read*

**The picture:** A hook is a fish hook. A small, sharp thing that catches attention and does not let go.

**Opening hooks (first lines or paragraph)**
- **A strange fact:** "The day my brother disappeared, the clocks kept running."
- **A voice:** a narrator with attitude.
- **A question:** something the reader needs answered.
- **A promise:** "This is the story of how I got grounded by a god."

**Chapter-end hooks**
- End on a **cliffhanger**: a choice, a reveal, danger.
- End on a **new question**.
- End on a **small surprise** that changes how we read what came before.
- Not every chapter needs a big one. Vary them, or readers feel tricked.

**The Z-shape teaser (for the blurb or first page)**
Think of your eyes tracing a Z: top-left to top-right (the premise), diagonal down (the problem), bottom-left to bottom-right (the stakes and the question). Give a teaser that follows that path: who, what trouble, what is at risk.

**Rules**
- **Pay off what you hook.** A hook with no payoff is clickbait, and readers remember.
- **Do not hook with lies.** Surprise is good. Cheating is not.

**Percy Jackson check:** Chapter titles are tiny hooks on their own, and many chapters end with a new threat or a revealed secret. The first page tells you flat out that this will be a bumpy ride.

**Try it (20 min):** Write five different first lines for your story. Read them to someone. Ask which one made them want line two.
