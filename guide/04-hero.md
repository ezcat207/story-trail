# Hero, Want, and Cost: The Engine
*5 min read*

**The picture:** A car needs fuel and a destination. Your hero's **want** is the destination. The **cost of failing** is the fuel, because it is the reason the hero keeps driving when the road gets bad.

**The rules**
- **A want must be specific.** "Be happy" is a wish. "Win the regional robotics trophy so Dad notices me" is a want.
- **Give a reason they cannot walk away.** If the hero can quit with no consequences, the reader can too.
- **Wants and needs are different.** The want is the thing they chase. The need is what they actually have to learn. Great stories let them chase one and find the other.
- **Make the hero act.** Heroes who just react to things happening are passengers. Let them make choices, even wrong ones.
- **Flaws are handles.** A small flaw gives the reader something to hold and gives the story something to break.

**Percy Jackson check:** Percy wants to clear his name and save his mom. He cannot walk away: his mom has been taken, and a war is coming. His flaw, a hot temper and a reckless streak, causes real problems and also saves the day. What he needs, to trust his friends and accept who he is, is what he finds along the way.

**Try it (20 min):** Fill in: "My hero wants ___ because ___. If they fail, they lose ___. Their flaw is ___." If you cannot fill all four, the hero is not ready.
